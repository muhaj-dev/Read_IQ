# The Electron Transport Chain

Subject: Biology

The electron transport chain is a series of protein complexes embedded in a
membrane that pass electrons from one carrier to the next.

The electron transport chain depends directly on redox reactions from
chemistry. Every step in the chain is an oxidation and a reduction: each
carrier is reduced when it accepts electrons and oxidised when it passes them
on. If redox reactions are unclear, the electron transport chain cannot be
understood properly.

As electrons move along the chain they release energy. The complexes use that
energy to pump protons across the membrane, which builds a proton gradient.
Building the proton gradient also requires understanding the electrochemical
gradient.

The electron transport chain appears in both cellular respiration and
photosynthesis. It is the shared machinery behind both, which is why it is
worth learning once and properly.

The final electron acceptor differs. In respiration it is oxygen, which is why
we breathe. In photosynthesis the electrons end up on NADP+ to form NADPH.
