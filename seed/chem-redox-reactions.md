# Redox Reactions

Subject: Chemistry

Redox reactions are reactions where electrons transfer between species.
Redox reactions depend on understanding valence electrons and electronegativity
first.

Oxidation is the loss of electrons. Reduction is the gain of electrons.
The mnemonic is OIL RIG: Oxidation Is Loss, Reduction Is Gain.

Oxidation and reduction always happen together. If one species loses electrons,
another must gain them. The species that gives electrons away is the reducing
agent; the one that accepts them is the oxidising agent.

Electrons move from species that hold them loosely to species that hold them
tightly. Each step releases energy, and the size of that release depends on how
far the electron falls.

This is the single most important idea for biology. The electron transport
chain is built from redox reactions: it is a series of steps where electrons
are passed from carrier to carrier, releasing energy each time. Redox reactions
are therefore a prerequisite for the electron transport chain, and through it
for respiration and photosynthesis.
