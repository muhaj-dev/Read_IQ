# Cellular Respiration — Week 5

Subject: Biology

Cellular respiration releases energy from glucose. Like photosynthesis, it
relies on an electron transport chain, so the electron transport chain is a
shared prerequisite for both topics.

Glycolysis splits glucose into pyruvate in the cytoplasm. Pyruvate then enters
the mitochondrion for the Krebs cycle.

The Krebs cycle (also called the citric acid cycle) produces NADH and FADH2.
These carry electrons to the electron transport chain in the inner mitochondrial
membrane.

The electron transport chain builds a proton gradient, and ATP synthase uses
that gradient to make ATP. This is the same mechanism as in photosynthesis,
which is why understanding the electron transport chain matters for both.
