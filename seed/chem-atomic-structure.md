# Atomic Structure and Electrons

Subject: Chemistry

Everything about bonding and reactions comes back to where electrons sit, so
this is the foundation you need before anything else in the course.

An atom has a nucleus of protons and neutrons, with electrons occupying shells
around it. Electrons in the outermost shell are called valence electrons, and
they are the ones that take part in reactions.

Atoms are most stable with a full outer shell. That single fact explains why
atoms give away, take, or share electrons.

Understanding valence electrons is a prerequisite for understanding redox
reactions, because redox reactions are defined entirely by electrons moving
from one atom to another. If you are unclear on which electrons are available
to move, redox reactions will not make sense.

Electronegativity measures how strongly an atom pulls electrons toward itself.
Oxygen is highly electronegative, which is why oxygen so often ends up taking
electrons in biological reactions.
