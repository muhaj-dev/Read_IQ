# Photosynthesis — Week 4

Subject: Biology

## Overview

Photosynthesis converts light energy into chemical energy stored in glucose.
It happens in two connected stages: the light-dependent reactions and the
Calvin cycle. The Calvin cycle cannot run without the products of the light
reactions, so the light reactions must be understood first.

## Light-dependent reactions

These take place in the thylakoid membrane. Chlorophyll absorbs photons and
excites electrons, which pass along the electron transport chain. Understanding
the electron transport chain is a prerequisite for understanding ATP synthesis,
because the chain is what builds the proton gradient.

The proton gradient across the thylakoid membrane drives ATP synthase. This
produces ATP and NADPH.

## ATP synthesis

ATP synthase uses the proton gradient created by the electron transport chain
to phosphorylate ADP into ATP. Without a functioning electron transport chain
there is no gradient, and therefore no ATP.

## The Calvin cycle

The Calvin cycle takes place in the stroma. It depends on both ATP and NADPH
produced by the light reactions. The enzyme RuBisCO fixes carbon dioxide onto
RuBP. The cycle then reduces the resulting molecules using ATP and NADPH to
form G3P, which is used to build glucose.

Understanding the Calvin cycle requires understanding ATP synthesis, because
every reduction step in the cycle is paid for with ATP.

The Calvin cycle is sometimes called the light-independent reaction, but it
still requires the light reactions to have run first.
