# The Krebs Cycle

Subject: Biology

The Krebs cycle, also called the citric acid cycle, runs in the mitochondrial
matrix. It depends on glycolysis, because it starts from pyruvate.

Acetyl-CoA joins with oxaloacetate to form citrate. The cycle then releases two
carbon dioxide molecules and regenerates oxaloacetate.

Each turn produces three NADH, one FADH2, and one ATP.

The point of the cycle is not the ATP. It is the NADH and FADH2, which carry
electrons to the electron transport chain. Understanding what the Krebs cycle
is for therefore requires understanding the electron transport chain and redox reactions.

Understanding the Krebs cycle requires understanding redox reactions, because
every electron carrier it fills is reduced in a redox reaction.

Students often memorise the intermediates and still miss this. The cycle exists
to harvest electrons.
