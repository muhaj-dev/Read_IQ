# Light-Dependent Reactions

Subject: Biology

The light-dependent reactions take place in the thylakoid membrane and they
depend on the electron transport chain.

Chlorophyll in photosystem II absorbs light and excites electrons. Water is
split to replace those electrons, and this is where the oxygen we breathe
comes from.

The excited electrons pass along an electron transport chain, and the energy
released pumps protons into the thylakoid space. That proton gradient drives
ATP synthase, exactly as in respiration.

Photosystem I re-excites the electrons and they are finally used to reduce
NADP+ into NADPH.

The outputs are ATP and NADPH, and both are required by the Calvin cycle.
